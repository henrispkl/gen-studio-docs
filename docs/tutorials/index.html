Tutorials

Learn by doing. The path goes from first launch to a small scene in Godot, about two hours in all; each part ends with something you made and takes 10 to 20 minutes.

The learning path​

1 First launchYou set Gen Studio up, learn where things are in the window, and make one picture: the concept art of a market cart, with two takes to choose from. About 10 minutes, plus the downloads, which run in the background.2 Your first propYou make a brass lantern: concept art, a 3D model, a game mesh inside your target look's budget, and then the file in a Godot project. About 15 minutes, most of it waiting for the models.3 A character that movesYou make a courier for the village: concept, four views, a 3D model, a game mesh, a skeleton, and a walk clip that plays in Godot. About 20 minutes, most of it the model and rig steps.4 Textures, shaders and effectsYou make three things that aren't models: a cobblestone texture that tiles, with its maps and a Godot material; a pond-water shader that Godot compiles; and a blue flame as an animated flipbook. About 15 minutes.5 Sound and musicYou give the village its sound: two sound effects with variants, a looping theme, and a waltz written note by note that you can edit bar by bar. Then you clean one sound up and run it through effects. About 15 minutes.6 Voices and wordsYou design a voice for the village's potion seller, write a haggling scene as a dialogue file for Godot, write an item's text, and then have the scene spoken line by line. About 10 minutes.7 A small scene in GodotYou export everything from the earlier tutorials in one go, add the Gen Studio dock to the Godot editor, and build a small scene: the courier walking on a cobbled street beside a lantern, a pond, a blue flame, with the theme playing. About 15 minutes.8 Work fasterYou do what the earlier tutorials did without the window: one asset from a terminal, a file of jobs that runs overnight, and an agent that makes, looks and picks for you. About 15 minutes at the keyboard. The batch runs while you're away.

More to try​

Once you know the window, in any order.

An anime character with sprites and a 2D rigYou define an anime character once, take shots of her, then make a visual novel sprite set and a 2D rig for Godot. Noa, the alchemist on these pages, was made this way. About 30 minutes without her own LoRA. Training one adds 20 to 40 minutes. Pixel art and chiptuneYou turn on the pixel pack and make a small retro set: a potion sprite, a knight, a key icon, a sheet of meadow tiles, an overworld tune and a coin sound. The pictures are true pixels on a limited palette, not a big picture made to look blocky. About 15 minutes. A base body and an outfitYou make one plain body, dress it in pieces that fit over each other, save them as an outfit, and swap outfits at runtime in Godot. About 60 minutes for the body and four pieces. Most of that is the model and rig steps, which run without you. A clip from your phoneYou film yourself doing a move and put it on your character as a clip. About 5 minutes, plus the filming. A shader from a descriptionYou describe a shader in a sentence and get a .gdshader that compiles in Godot, with sliders for what you want to tweak. Then you read the judge's notes and steer a retake. About 5 minutes. Working with Claude Code over MCPYou connect Claude Code to a project and have it run Gen Studio for you: make assets, look at the takes, pick, retake and export. About 10 minutes.
