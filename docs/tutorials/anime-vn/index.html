An anime character with sprites and a 2D rig

You define an anime character once, take shots of her, then make a visual novel sprite set and a 2D rig for Godot. Noa, the alchemist on these pages, was made this way. About 30 minutes without her own LoRA. Training one adds 20 to 40 minutes.

You'll make:

  • a character sheet, the definition every shot copies
  • shots of her: a transparent sprite and one in a scene
  • a sprite set with expressions, for Ren'Py and Godot
  • a 2D rig with three animations

You'll learn:

  • how one asset (a character) feeds others (shots, sets, rigs)
  • framing, pose and background on a shot
  • the rim light, and why you reroll it

Before you start​

  • Do first launch and your first prop.
  • The anime pack is off by default. Turn it on in Settings → Extensions → gen.anime, restart the app, and install its models:
gen ext enable gen.anime
gen install weights --group anime

Define the character​

  1. Press All kinds…, then Anime and Anime character.

  2. Describe her in Danbooru tags: who she is, hair, eyes, build and outfit. Noa's are:

    1girl, solo, alchemist, short silver hair, bob cut, single long braid, teal eyes, small round earrings,
    dark navy long coat with brass buttons, rolled sleeves, cream knit sweater, leather belt with potion vials,
    brown shorts, black tights, short lace-up boots, brass goggles around neck

  3. Name the asset noa and press Generate.

Checkpoint

the Character sheet step shows her full body on white.

Tags keep a character close, not exact. The optional Character LoRA step trains a small add-on for the drawing model on pictures of her. It takes 20 to 40 minutes and the whole GPU. Every shot of her uses it from then on.

Shots​

  1. Press All kinds…, then Anime and Shot of a character.

  2. Pick the Character. Set Framing to knee-up, the usual sprite, and Pose to standing.

  3. Set Background to Transparent for a sprite.

  4. Write the rest in Shot:

    gentle smile, looking at viewer, arms at sides

  5. Name it noa_up and press Generate.

  6. Make a second shot, noa_lanterns, with Background set to A scene:

    walking through a mountain village street at dusk, paper lanterns, festival stalls, looking back over shoulder, soft smile

Checkpoint

two Shot assets. Each rail starts with the character, marked source: her picture to look at, nothing to change there.

Up to four characters fit in one shot. With three or more, each is redrawn in its own place (Layout), so they don't swap clothes.

The rim light​

The optional Rim light step paints a bright, thin rim on the edges facing a light, like fighting-game key art.

  1. Open a shot and press Add step at the end of the rail. Pick Rim light.
  2. Pick the Rim colour and Light from.
  3. Set how strong it is. The default model has Intensity, 1 to 5: 1 is a crisp, thin rim. With Model set to Qwen-Image-Edit 2511 the setting is Strength, and Keep only the rim colour drops anything else the relight painted.
  4. Press New take.

Checkpoint

the take shows the lit picture, with her edges on the light's side glowing.

The result changes with the seed. If the rim is faint, right-click the take and press Reroll: a new seed that replaces the take.

gen retake noa_up anime_rim --set intensity=2
gen reroll noa_up anime_rim

A sprite set​

  1. Press All kinds…, then Anime and Sprite or portrait set.
  2. Pick the knee-up Shot, noa_up.
  3. Write the Expressions you want: neutral, happy, angry, surprised, sad, smug.
  4. Name it noa_set and press Generate.
Checkpoint

one picture per expression, as at the top of this page. Each has talking and blinking frames.

You also get a faceset, and files for Ren'Py (.rpy) and Godot (sprite_frames.tres with portrait.gd).

A 2D rig​

  1. Press All kinds…, then Anime and 2D rig (cut-out animation).
  2. Pick the same transparent shot, name it noa_rig, and press Generate.
  3. Press Export to Godot and open noa_rig.tscn in Godot.
Checkpoint

the AnimationPlayer plays idle on start: she breathes, and her back hair swings.

The shot is split into parts (hair, face, arms, legs, torso), the hidden areas are filled in, and you get a Godot scene with bones and three animations: idle breathing, a sway and a head tilt.

What you learned​

  • A character defined once, then shot in any framing.
  • Her own LoRA for an exact look.
  • The rim light, sprite sets and 2D rigs.

Next​

Pixel art and chiptune, or go back to the tutorials.

If it went wrong​

  • The anime kinds don't show: see troubleshooting.
  • The rim is faint or lands in the wrong place: reroll the take.
  • A hand or a face is off in a shot: retouch a picture.
