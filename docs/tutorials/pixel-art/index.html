Pixel art and chiptune

You turn on the pixel pack and make a small retro set: a potion sprite, a knight, a key icon, a sheet of meadow tiles, an overworld tune and a coin sound. The pictures are true pixels on a limited palette, not a big picture made to look blocky. About 15 minutes.

You'll make:

  • potion, tiny_knight and brass_key: sprites at their real size, with enlarged previews
  • meadow_tiles, a tile sheet with one palette
  • overworld, a chip-flavoured track, and coin, a retro sound effect

You'll learn:

  • the two steps of every pixel picture: the drawing, then Pixelate
  • that Pixelate runs no model, so sizes and palettes are quick to try
  • the two ways to chip music

Before you start​

  • Do first launch and your first prop.
  • The pixel pack, gen.pixel, ships off. Turn it on in Settings → Extensions, then restart the app. Or:
gen ext enable gen.pixel

Its models download the first time a step needs them, after the app asks.

A sprite​

  1. Press All kinds…, then Pixel art & chiptune and Pixel sprite.

  2. Write this in Sprite:

    a round glass potion bottle with glowing blue liquid and a cork

  3. Type potion in Asset name and press Generate. One subject, plainly described. The pixel-art style and the plain background are added for you.

  4. The first step, Drawing, is a normal-sized picture in a pixel style. Retake it until the shape is right.

  5. Press Use and run Pixelate, or Run next: Pixelate in the ⋯ menu when the take is already in use.

Checkpoint

the rail reads Drawing, Pixelate. The Pixelate take is the bottle cropped to its outline on a transparent background.

Sizes and palettes​

Pixelate works on the CPU and takes seconds, so try things here instead of redrawing.

  1. On Pixelate, set Size to 32 px and press Retake. Size is the longest side in real pixels. 48 keeps most drawings readable, 32 suits simple subjects.
  2. Set Palette to PICO-8 and retake again. A fixed palette matches each colour to the console's nearest one.
  3. Go back to Free (from the drawing) and lower Colours to 8. Small bright accents stay in the palette.
  4. Tick two or three takes in the strip and press Compare 3 (the button counts what you ticked) to see them side by side. Use the one you want.
Checkpoint

several Pixelate takes of the same drawing, each a different size or palette.

Clean outline makes a dark outline continuous and one pixel wide. It adds none to a drawing without one. In the take's folder, sprite.png is the real-size picture for your game and preview.png is the same one enlarged. In Godot, set the texture filter to Nearest.

A character, an icon, tiles​

The other picture kinds are the same two steps.

  1. Pixel character, a full body. Name it tiny_knight:

    a small knight with a red plume, silver armour, round shield and short sword, front view

  2. Pixel icon, for items and UI. Name it brass_key:

    an ornate brass key with a heart-shaped bow

  3. Pixel tiles, a sheet cut at the tiles' borders, 16 or 32 px, with one palette. Name it meadow_tiles:

    grass meadow with a dirt path, small flowers and stones, top-down

Checkpoint

four pixel assets in the project, each with a Drawing and its pixel version.

# knight.yaml, run with: gen run knight.yaml
asset: tiny_knight
kind: pixel_character
prompt: a small knight with a red plume, silver armour, round shield and short sword, front view

A chiptune and a coin​

  1. Press All kinds…, then Pixel art & chiptune and Chiptune.

  2. Write the mood and use in Style. The chip's tags are added for you.

    cheerful overworld theme, bouncy lead, driving bass, adventure

  3. Leave Sound on NES. Set Length (seconds) to 45 and BPM to 150.

  4. Type overworld in Asset name and press Generate.

  5. Now the coin: Retro sound effect, with Sound on coin. Name it coin and press Generate. No model runs: a generator makes it in a moment.

  6. Play the coin's variants and press Pick this one on the ones you keep. The first is the classic sound, the others are close relatives.

Checkpoint

a looping track that sounds like an old console, and a coin sound with four variants.

The Chiptune step asks a music model to imitate a console. For the real chip voices, take any Music track, add Stems, then MIDI, then Chip render: it keeps the song and changes the band, playing the notes on the Era you pick. Crush on a chiptune makes it harsher and closer to the hardware.

What you learned​

  • A pixel picture is drawn large, then reduced to real pixels and a palette.
  • Retake Pixelate, not the drawing, to change size, colours or palette.
  • Chiptune imitates a console, Chip render plays real chip voices from a track's notes, and retro sounds come from a generator.

If it went wrong​

  • The pixel kinds don't show in New asset: the pack is off, or the app wasn't restarted. See extensions on and off.
  • "Needs a download": see troubleshooting.
  • The sprite looks blurry in Godot: set the texture filter to Nearest.
  • Chip render's notes land off the beat: set BPM on the step yourself. See stems and MIDI.
