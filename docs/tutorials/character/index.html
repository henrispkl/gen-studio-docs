A character that moves

You make a courier for the village: concept, four views, a 3D model, a game mesh, a skeleton, and a walk clip that plays in Godot. About 20 minutes, most of it the model and rig steps.

You'll make:

  • courier, a rigged character with bones named for Godot's humanoid profile
  • a looping walk clip inside its .glb

You'll learn:

  • a character's steps: Concept, Turnaround, High poly, Game mesh, Rig
  • what a review is, and how to check a rig before you go on
  • clips, which sit on their own row of the rail

Before you start​

  • Do your first prop. This tutorial uses the same project, takes and export.
  • The clip step at Fast, Balanced and Quality needs a motion model. If it isn't installed, the app asks before it downloads.

Describe the character​

  1. Press All kinds… on Home, then Characters & bodies and 3D game character.

  2. Write this in Prompt:

    a young courier with short auburn hair, goggles on her forehead, a short teal jacket over a cream shirt, brown trousers, sturdy boots, arms held away from the body

  3. Type courier in Asset name and press Generate.

  4. Make a second take with Retake if the first isn't right. Click the one you want.

The concept is drawn in an A-pose with the arms away from the body, so it builds cleanly in 3D. Keep held things out of the prompt: a sword fused to a hand can't be rigged. Make it a prop.

Checkpoint

the Concept step shows the whole courier, head to boots, arms away from her sides, on a plain background.

The four views​

  1. With your concept take picked, press Use and run Turnaround. If the take is already in use, open the ⋯ menu in the same bar and press Run next: Turnaround.

  2. When it finishes, check the four pictures: front, left, back, right.

  3. Look for two things. Left and right aren't swapped: a detail on one side, like a pocket, stays on that side. And all four show the same character, with nothing added or dropped.

  4. If a view is wrong, press Retake. The step warns you when it can't tell left from right.

Checkpoint

four views of one courier, at fixed quarter turns. The 3D model is only as good as these.

The model and the rig​

  1. Press Run to end. Three steps run:

    • High poly builds the dense model from the four views.
    • Game mesh fits it to your target look's budget.
    • Rig adds the skeleton and the skin weights in one pass.

  2. Rig is a review: the run stops there for you to check. Click Rig, then Open large.

  3. Press Bones (or B) to see the skeleton inside the body. Turn the model and check the joints sit where they should: shoulders, elbows, hips, knees.

  4. When it looks right, close the viewer and press Continue in the toolbar.

Check the rig before you make clips. If a joint is off, Edit joints in the viewer lets you move it, and the Rig step's Keep the skeleton (re-skin only) setting then redoes only the weights. Fixing a joint after you have clips means making the clips again.

Checkpoint

the rail reads Concept, Turnaround, High poly, Game mesh, Rig, all finished.

Long hair or a cape?

The Rig step's Cloth setting (Auto) adds bone chains down long hair, so it swings in Godot. See cloth presets. Capes and hats are wearables.

A walk​

  1. Press ⋮ in the toolbar, then New clip.

  2. Leave Make on A motion. Type walk in Clip name.

  3. Write this in Describe the motion:

    a person walks forward at a steady pace

  4. Leave Length at 3 seconds and press Generate.

  5. The clip gets its own row on the rail. When it's done, open it large and press play.

One action per clip works best. A clip loops and stays in place by default: the end meets the start and the hips don't wander, so your game moves the character, not the animation. A job file can turn those off:

# walk.yaml, run with: gen run walk.yaml
asset: courier
kind: motion
clip: walk
prompt: a person walks forward at a steady pace

Add loop: false or in_place: false to change the defaults.

Checkpoint

the rail has a second row with the walk clip, and the courier walks on the spot in the viewer.

Into Godot​

  1. Press Export to Godot.
  2. In Godot, open res://assets/gen/courier/ and drag the .glb into a scene.
  3. Select its AnimationPlayer. The walk is in the list, and Godot loops clips whose names end in _loop.
Checkpoint

the courier walks in your Godot scene.

What you learned​

  • A character is a prop's idea with two more needs: four matching views, and a skeleton.
  • A review stops the run until you press Continue.
  • Clips are made after the rig, each on its own row, and travel inside the character's .glb.

If it went wrong​

  • Left and right are swapped: see troubleshooting.
  • The clip slides, or the feet float: see troubleshooting.
  • Hair doesn't move, or cloth explodes: see troubleshooting and cloth presets.
  • "Needs a download": see troubleshooting.
