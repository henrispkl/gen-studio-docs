Your first prop

You make a brass lantern: concept art, a 3D model, a game mesh inside your target look's budget, and then the file in a Godot project. About 15 minutes, most of it waiting for the models.

You'll make:

  • lantern, a prop with a baked texture, as a .glb in Godot

You'll learn:

  • a prop's steps: Concept, Prepped cut-out, Mesh, Game mesh
  • Use and run, Run to end and Re-run stale
  • the big 3D viewer
  • Export to Godot

Before you start​

  • Do first launch: you need the project from it.
  • A Godot 4 project somewhere on disk: a folder with project.godot. An empty new project is fine.
  • Blender on your PATH. The Game mesh step uses it, and gen doctor says whether it's found.

Describe it​

  1. Press All kinds… on Home (or Ctrl+N), then Props & scenery and 3D prop.

  2. Write this in Prompt:

    a brass alchemist's lantern with blue glass panes and a ring handle

  3. Type lantern in Asset name.

  4. Press Generate.

Say what it is, what it's made of and how worn it is. The camera and the plain background are added for you. The sparkle on the prompt rewrites it the way the step's model likes it.

Checkpoint

the lantern opens with one step on the rail, Concept, and its first take appears in the middle.

From the command line, in the project folder, this does the same and runs every step:

gen make prop "a brass alchemist's lantern with blue glass panes and a ring handle" --asset lantern --seed 5

Choose the concept​

Fixing a shape in the picture is quicker than fixing it in 3D, so get the concept right first.

  1. Press Retake once or twice. Each take gets a new seed, and the old ones stay.

  2. Click through the takes in the strip. Look for a clean outline, all four sides plausible, and nothing fused to the lantern.

  3. Click the take you want, then press Use and run Prepped cut-out. It makes this take the one in use and starts the next step from it. If your choice is already in use, the button isn't there: go straight on to Run to end.

Checkpoint

a second pill, Prepped cut-out, is on the rail. It shows the lantern cut out of its background.

Run to the end​

  1. Press Run to end in the toolbar.
  2. Watch the rail. New steps appear as they start:
    • Mesh builds a dense 3D model from the cut-out. This is the long one.
    • Game mesh rebuilds it inside your target look's triangle budget and bakes the texture.
  3. While it runs, press Queue in the bottom bar to see what's running and what waits.
Checkpoint

the rail reads Concept, Prepped cut-out, Mesh, Game mesh, each with a finished dot. Nothing is marked stale.

Look at it​

  1. Click Game mesh on the rail, then Open large (or double-click the model).

  2. Drag to turn it, scroll to zoom. Press 3 for the wireframe, 7 for the UV checker and 1 to go back to shaded. H puts a 1.8 m figure beside it for scale.

  3. Read the side panel: Stats and Budget show the triangles against what the target look allows.

Not happy with it? Go back to Concept and use another take. The steps built from the old one turn stale: still there, but out of date. Re-run stale in the toolbar rebuilds them. To change only the mesh, set Triangle budget or Texture size on Game mesh and press Retake.

Checkpoint

the model in the viewer looks like your concept from every side, and the budget isn't exceeded.

Put it in Godot​

  1. Press Export to Godot in the toolbar.
  2. The first time, pick your Godot project's folder, the one that holds project.godot. Gen Studio remembers it. Shift-click the button to pick another.
  3. Open the project in Godot. The lantern is in res://assets/gen/lantern/.
  4. Drag the .glb into a 3D scene.
gen export lantern ~/games/lantern-village
Checkpoint

the lantern stands in your Godot scene with its texture.

What you learned​

  • A prop is four steps, and each is built from the take in use of the one before.
  • Use and run picks a take and goes on. Run to end does the rest.
  • Changing the take in use makes later steps stale, and Re-run stale rebuilds them.
  • Export copies the takes in use into res://assets/gen/<asset>/. Export again after a retake and the files are replaced in place.

If it went wrong​

  • Parts are missing from the model (the ring handle, thin bars): see troubleshooting.
  • It looks wrong in Godot: see troubleshooting.
  • Export refuses: stale steps or the wrong folder. See troubleshooting and export to Godot.
  • More on takes: retake and compare and run the stale steps.
