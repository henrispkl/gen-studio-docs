A shader from a description

Describe a shader in a sentence and get a .gdshader that compiles in Godot, with sliders for what you want to tweak. About 2 minutes at Fast.

1. Describe it​

Press All kinds…, then Shader. Say where it goes, what it looks like and what you want to change later:

a flickering green witchlight glow with a soft pulsing core and a thin bright rim; tweakable colour and pulse speed

Press Generate.

2. Written, compiled, judged​

Gemma writes the shader and Godot compiles it. Compile errors go back to Gemma until it compiles. Then Gemma looks at a preview and fixes what's missing: up to eight rounds at Balanced. The take keeps the best-scored round. Its notes are in the take's judge.json.

Each uniform with a hint_range is a slider in Godot's inspector.

Not quite right?

Retake with a clearer description. Name the surface (2D sprite or 3D mesh), the colours and what moves.

3. Use it in Godot​

Press Export to Godot, then make a ShaderMaterial on your mesh or sprite and pick the .gdshader. Set its uniforms in the inspector.

Let Claude Code judge​

With Settings → Extensions → Who reviews a shader's look set to Claude Code, looks wait for Claude Code's verdict instead of Gemma's. See Claude Code over MCP.

What you learned​

  • Shaders written and compiled for you, with sliders.
  • The judge rounds, and how to steer a retake.
