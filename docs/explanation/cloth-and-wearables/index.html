Wearables, fitting and cloth

How does a piece of clothing end up fitting one character and moving with it?

A wearable is a piece of armour, clothing or an accessory made for one rigged character or base body. It comes out as its own game-ready mesh that fits the body and follows every clip.

Why not generate the piece in 3D directly?​

No open model builds a garment onto a given body mesh. What does work: image models are good at "the same character, now wearing X", multiview 3D models build a mesh from fixed views, and fitting a mesh to a body and copying skin weights can be done with plain, tested code. So a wearable is made in ordinary steps, each with takes: Design, Mesh, Fit, an optional Paint, and Skin.

An example: the courier's hat​

The sample project has a character, the courier, and a wearable made for her: "a wide-brimmed felt courier hat with a feather".

Design. The courier is rendered in her rest pose from the front, left, back and right. The hat is drawn onto the front view first, then onto the others with the finished front as a reference, so all four show the same hat. The hat is cut out of each view by its description, and the four cut-outs are checked against each other in 3D. A view that disagrees is redrawn. The step stops here for your review.

Mesh. The Route field picks how the piece is built. A piece that hugs the body (boots, gloves, shirts) is made from the body's own surface, so it fits by construction. A piece that stands off it, like a helmet or this hat, is built in 3D from the four cut-outs. A cape is its back-view outline hung behind the body. On Auto the route is chosen for you; set it yourself if the take comes out wrong.

Fit. The hat is placed where its cut-outs say, hugged onto the head without losing its shape, then pushed out to the Clearance so the head can't poke through.

Skin. Each point of the hat copies the skin weights of the closest point of the courier's body where distance and direction agree, and the rest are filled in smoothly. It's exported on her skeleton, so it follows every clip she has. As a check she's posed (arms up, knees bent, a twist) and any body points that poke through are counted.

Layers​

A piece on a higher Layer (a coat, 2) is fitted over the same character's pieces on lower layers (a shirt, 1). One piece per Slot and layer is worn at a time.

Cloth: bone chains, swung by Godot​

Capes, skirts, long hair and tails should swing. Gen Studio doesn't bake a cloth simulation into the clips. It adds bone chains down the parts that hang, and Godot's SpringBoneSimulator3D swings them while the game runs, so the cloth reacts to whatever the character does, not only to the clips you made.

Which part hangs comes from the Cloth field, or on Auto from the piece's words (cape, skirt, coat, robe, scarf). How it swings comes from Cloth material: cloth, leather, hair or chain. A character's own long hair gets the same treatment on its Rig step.

Before a Skin take is kept, Godot swings the chains without a window. If they stretch more than 5 %, the take fails and says so.

What it means in practice​

  • A wearable belongs to one body. Make it again for another character.
  • The character needs its Rig before you make a piece for it.
  • Describe one piece at a time: "leather boots", not a whole outfit.
  • Make lower layers first, so the coat is fitted over the shirt.
  • If a piece pokes through, raise Clearance and retake.
  • Design takes a few minutes at any profile: four views are drawn one after another.
