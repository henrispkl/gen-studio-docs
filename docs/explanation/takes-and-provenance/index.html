Takes, stale steps and provenance

What happens to a result when you make another one, and how can any result be made again?

Nothing is overwritten​

Every time a step runs, it writes a new folder. That's a take. The app calls it Take 2, the folder is v002:

<project>/assets/lantern/steps/concept/v001/
                                       v002/
                         steps/retopo/v001/

One take per step is in use. Which one is written in the asset's canonical.json. The other takes stay where they are, so you can go back at any time. Using a different take changes one line in that file and moves nothing.

The first take of a step is used straight away. A take you make later, with Retake, New take or gen retake, is kept beside it and not used until you choose it with Use this take or gen promote.

An example: the lantern​

The sample project's lantern is a prop: Concept, Prepped cut-out, Mesh, Game mesh. All four ran from the first concept. You retake the Concept with "blue glass panes" moved to the front of the prompt, like Take 2 better, and use it.

The cut-out, the mesh and the game mesh were built from Take 1. Each take records its parents, the takes in use on the step before it when it ran, so the app knows they no longer match. They're stale (dashed in the diagram). Re-run stale, or gen rerun-stale lantern, makes a new take of each from the takes now in use, in order. If you go back to Concept Take 1 instead, nothing is stale any more.

A take built on another take of the same step (Build on Take 2, on Retouch or Edit) records that take's parents, since that's where its picture came from. Re-run stale leaves such a take marked for you to rebuild by hand.

What a take records​

Each take folder has its outputs (concept.png, high.glb, low.glb, sfx.ogg) and a meta.json with the fully resolved job:

  • the step, the profile and the target look it ran at, and any fallback to a lighter profile;
  • the model, its extension and worker versions, and its weights pinned to a full 40-character Hugging Face revision;
  • for ComfyUI steps, the workflow template's sha256 and the ComfyUI and custom-node commits;
  • every value of the step's form, the seed, and the rewritten prompt if you asked for one;
  • the parent takes;
  • timings, and the licence of the model and of any imported file.

Where the file format allows, the job is also embedded in the output itself (PNG text chunks, glTF extras), so a file you drag out of the folder keeps its history.

This record is the take's provenance. gen run --from <meta.json> re-resolves a take's job from it and checks that it comes out the same. A seed alone wouldn't do that: the same seed with another model revision or a changed workflow gives a different picture. If a result can't be remade from its meta.json, that's a bug.

Folders are the truth​

gen.db is an index of jobs and assets that makes the app fast. The folders are the source of truth. gen doctor --reindex rebuilds the index from them.

What it means in practice​

  • Retake freely. A new take never replaces the one in use until you say so.
  • Compare takes of one step side by side, then use the one you like.
  • Stale steps are a to-do, not a loss. Export refuses a stale asset until they've run.
  • Discard… and Reroll move takes to the project's .trash/ folder. Gen Studio doesn't empty it. You do, when you're sure.
  • You can copy a project folder to another machine and every take and choice comes with it.
  • meta.json doubles as a credits log: the licence of every model and imported file your game uses.
