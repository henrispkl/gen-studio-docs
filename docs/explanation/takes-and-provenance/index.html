Takes, stale steps and provenance

Gen Studio never overwrites a result. Every time a step runs, it writes a new folder. Here's what's in those folders, how the app decides which results belong together, and why every result can be made again.

A step, its takes and the one in use​

An asset follows a pipeline: an ordered line of steps. A prop goes Concept → Prepped cut-out → Mesh → Game mesh, with an optional Paint at the end. A character adds a Turnaround and a Rig, then a row of steps per clip.

Each run of a step makes a take, in its own folder. The app calls it Take 3, the folder is v003:

<project>/assets/chest/steps/concept/v001/
                                    v002/
                                    v003/
                       steps/retopo/v001/

You use one take per step. Which one is written in the asset's canonical.json. The others stay where they are, so you can go back at any time. Using a different take changes one line, not any files.

A take made from the app with Retake or New take, or with gen retake, is kept but not used until you choose it ( Use this take, or gen promote). The first take of a step is used straight away.

Why steps go stale​

Every take records its parents: the takes in use on the step before it (and any extra inputs, such as the rig a clip is put on) at the moment it ran. If you later use a different take of a parent step, the child was made from something you no longer use. It is stale.

Stale is not an error. The old results are still there and still valid for the old concept. It means "these don't match what you chose any more". A take built on another take of its step (Build on Take N, base) records that take's parents, since that's where its pixels came from, and keeps a hard link to its picture (base.png), so it still re-resolves when that take is discarded. Re-run stale leaves such a take marked: you rebuild it by hand. Re-run stale (or gen rerun-stale) makes new takes from the takes now in use, in order. If you change your mind and use the old take again, nothing is stale any more.

Export refuses a stale asset. That keeps Godot from getting a character whose rig was made from one mesh and whose texture came from another.

What meta.json holds​

Each take folder has its outputs (concept.png, high.glb, low.glb, sfx.ogg, …) and a meta.json with the fully resolved job:

  • the stage, the profile and the target look it ran at, and any fallback to a lighter profile;
  • the model, its extension and worker versions, and its weights pinned to a full 40-character Hugging Face revision;
  • for ComfyUI steps, the workflow template's sha256 and the ComfyUI and custom-node commits;
  • every value of the step's form, the seed, and the output of the prompt enhancer if it ran;
  • the parent takes and their files' hashes;
  • timings, and the licence of the model and of any imported file.

Where the file format allows, the job is also embedded in the output itself (PNG text chunks, glTF extras), so a file you drag out of the folder keeps its history.

Provenance: made again, the same​

gen run --from <meta.json> re-resolves a take's job from its meta.json and checks that it comes out the same: same model revision, same template, same values. The rule in the code is simple: if a result can't be remade from its meta.json, that is a bug.

A seed alone is not enough for that. A seed with a different model revision, a changed workflow template or another prompt rewrite gives a different picture. That is why everything is pinned and recorded, not only the seed.

meta.json doubles as a credits log. The model's licence and an imported asset's source and licence are in every take, so you can list what your game uses.

Folders are the truth​

gen.db is a SQLite index of jobs, assets and events. It makes the app fast, but it is not the source of truth: the folders are. If the database is lost or damaged, gen doctor --reindex rebuilds it from the asset folders. You can copy a project folder to another machine, open it, and every take, choice and history is there.

Folders also survive tools that know nothing about Gen Studio: Godot, Blender and agents can read them directly.

Nothing is deleted behind your back​

Discard…, Reroll and Move to .trash (Settings → Storage → Clean up) move takes to the project's .trash/ folder. Gen Studio never deletes them. Empty .trash/ yourself when you are sure.
