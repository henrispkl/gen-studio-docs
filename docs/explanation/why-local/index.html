Why local

Gen Studio runs every model on your own machine, on one GPU. It doesn't call an online service to make anything. Here's why, and what it costs.

Your work stays yours​

Prompts, reference pictures, imported models and every result stay on your disk. Nothing is uploaded to make an asset. The only network traffic is what you ask for: downloading models and runtimes, searching free asset libraries, and the phone page, which stays on your local network behind a token.

That matters for unreleased games, for work under NDA, and for anything you'd rather not hand to a service that may train on it.

Reproducible, years later​

Online services change their models without telling you. A prompt that gave a good result last month may not give it today, and there is no way back.

Locally, every model is pinned to an exact revision (a full 40-character Hugging Face commit), every ComfyUI workflow to its hash, and every input file to its hash. Each take's meta.json records all of it, so a take can be made again, the same, long after. gen update writes those pins into a gen.lock, and gen restore installs exactly them on another machine. See Takes, stale steps and provenance.

No per-image cost, no one else's queue​

Once the models are downloaded, a take costs only electricity. You can make twenty concepts to find one good one, or run a batch overnight, without counting credits. Nothing waits behind other people's jobs, and nothing stops working when a service changes its terms or shuts down.

Standalone​

Gen Studio installs and manages what it needs under one data folder: ComfyUI at a pinned commit with pinned custom nodes, a llama.cpp server for text, one Python environment per model worker, and the weights. It doesn't depend on anything else you have set up. If you already run your own ComfyUI or llama.cpp server, you can point Gen Studio at it in ~/.config/gen-studio/config.toml; it will use it but never stop it.

Licences are recorded, not hidden​

Local models come with a range of licences: some fully open, some non-commercial, some with regional limits. Gen Studio records the licence of the model behind every take, and the source and licence of every free asset you import, in the take itself. It doesn't decide for you, but you can always see what your game is built from before you ship it.

The costs​

Local has real costs:

  • A capable GPU. Gen Studio is built and tested on an RTX 4090 with 24 GB. Several steps want 16 to 24 GB of graphics memory. The VRAM scheduler runs one heavy model at a time and falls back to lighter profiles, but a small card limits what you can run at Quality.
  • Disk space. The Minimal tier is 80 GB, Recommended 266 GB, Full 358 GB, plus the environments. You can put models on another drive.
  • A first install. Downloading runtimes and weights takes time once. Downloads resume and are checked by hash.
  • Speed. A big hosted model may be faster per image than your card. Fast profiles keep the trying-out loop short; Quality is for the keepers.
