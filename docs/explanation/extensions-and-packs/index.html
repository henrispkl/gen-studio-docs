Extensions and packs

Where do the steps and models come from, and why can you switch them off?

The core runs no models​

Gen Studio's core owns the queue, the VRAM scheduler, the asset folders and the settings. Every step that makes something comes from an extension, also called a pack: a folder with a gen-extension.toml that lists its steps, what each profile runs, its form fields, and its model files pinned by exact revision and hash. The app reads that file without running any of the extension's code, so it can list a pack's steps, sizes and licences before anything is installed.

A pack can also bring asset kinds. Turn it off and its kinds, steps and downloads go away. Assets you made with it keep their folders, and come back when you turn it on.

An example: the potion and the pixel pack​

The sample project's potion is a pixel sprite. That kind comes from gen.pixel, which ships off. Until you switch it on in Settings → Extensions and restart, New asset has no pixel kinds at all. The anime shots are the same story with gen.anime. The festival waltz comes from gen.composer, which is on by default and needs no model.

Why each worker has its own environment​

Models are research code, and they pin different, conflicting versions of the same libraries. One shared Python environment would break each time a model was added. So a pack that brings its own model code runs it in a worker: a separate process with its own environment under the data folder, installed by gen install workers. The core talks to it over a small protocol and passes file paths, never the pictures themselves.

Three things follow. A crashed worker can't take the app down. Stopping the process frees all its VRAM. And adding a pack can't break one you already use.

Many packs need no worker of their own. They run on the managed ComfyUI, with workflow templates pinned by hash, or on the managed llama.cpp server for text.

Add-ons, Kits and libraries​

  • An add-on is an extension that offers another model for its parent's steps. The step then shows a Model choice beside Profile. It's on only while its parent is on.
  • A Kit is smaller than an extension: an optional recipe for one step, made of a kit.toml, a guide and a preview picture. It names a model family Gen Studio already has and holds no code, so installing one runs nothing. Without a Kit a step runs on Default.
  • A library is where model files and environments live. The data folder is the home library. You can add libraries on other drives and place a pack's models there. Gen Studio links them back into the data folder, so every step finds its files in one place. If a drive is missing, the step says which one and nothing is written to the empty mount point.

What it means in practice​

  • Turn off packs you don't use to keep New asset and Settings → Models short. Turning one off deletes nothing.
  • A switch applies when you restart the app.
  • Models take the space, not the packs. Put the big ones on another drive with a library.
  • A pack's models can download the first time a step needs them, after the app asks.
  • gen update writes the installed pins to gen.lock, and gen restore installs exactly those on another machine.
  • You can write your own pack. gen new extension scaffolds one and gen ext validate checks it.
