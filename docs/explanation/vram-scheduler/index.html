The VRAM scheduler

How do image, 3D, motion, sound and text models share one graphics card?

Most of these models want most of the card, so they take turns. The scheduler decides what is loaded, when it's swapped out, and what happens when something doesn't fit.

One heavy model at a time​

Every model runs in its own process: a model worker, the managed ComfyUI, or the managed llama.cpp server. Each declares how much VRAM it needs and how long it may sit idle before it's unloaded (5 minutes is typical).

When a job needs a model that isn't loaded, the scheduler unloads the others that can't share the card, then reads the real free VRAM from the driver before it starts the new one. Declared numbers are only a starting point. The queue row says what's happening ("waiting for 21 GB of free VRAM"), so a swap never looks like a hang.

While a step runs, Gen Studio samples how much memory its own processes hold. The peak of the last five runs is kept per step and profile, on your machine. After a step has run once, the fit badges and the scheduler use that measured number.

An example: the sample project's batch​

The sample project was made by one batch file: eight anime shots, a handful of props and characters, sounds, music and text. The queue prefers the job whose step ran last, so the eight shots run back to back with their model loaded once. When the lantern's Mesh comes up, the scheduler unloads the picture model, checks the free VRAM and loads the 3D one. The text jobs go to llama.cpp the same way. Nobody had to order the file by model.

When it doesn't fit​

  • A step that runs out of GPU memory is queued again one profile lighter: Quality, Balanced, Fast, Ultra fast. The take records each fallback, with the reason.
  • If free VRAM stays too low to start at all, the scheduler waits a short while, then fails with how much it needed and how much was free.
  • Games, browsers and other AI tools use VRAM too. The scheduler names them in a warning ("other programs are using the GPU: pid 4321 (3.2 GB)") and never stops a process it didn't start.

What it means in practice​

  • Queue as much as you like. Jobs wait their turn, and same-model jobs are grouped for you.
  • A swap between models costs load time. A batch of twenty concepts, then twenty meshes, loads each model once.
  • Close games and video before a heavy step, or let the step wait.
  • A take made at a lighter profile than you asked for says so. Retake it when the card is free.
  • If you point Gen Studio at your own ComfyUI or llama.cpp server in ~/.config/gen-studio/config.toml, it uses it and never stops it. Its VRAM is yours to manage.
