Steps and paths

How is an asset put together, and what happens to the rest when you change one part of it?

An asset is a row of steps​

An asset is one thing you make, and it's a folder in the project: assets/courier/. Its kind (prop, character, music, anime shot) decides the steps it starts with. A prop starts as Concept, Prepped cut-out, Mesh, Game mesh. A character is Concept, Turnaround, High poly, Game mesh, Rig. Some steps are optional and wait for you to start them: Paint and Frame in 3D on a model, Clean up and Effects on a sound.

Each step takes the result of the step before it and makes the next thing. Steps connect by what they pass on (a picture, a mesh, a rig, a clip, a sound, text), not by which model made it. That's why a profile can swap the model behind a step and the next step still works.

Every run of a step makes a take. One take per step is in use, and that's the one the next step builds from. The takes page covers what a take holds.

The step list belongs to the asset​

The kind gives an asset its first steps, but the list is the asset's own, written in its asset.json: each step, and the step it comes after. So you can add a step to the end of a row with the dashed +, and two assets of one kind can end up with different steps.

Paths: another route from the same step​

A path (a branch) starts from any step of the first row and runs beside it, with its own steps and takes. The app names them b1, b2, and you can rename them. A character's clips work the same way: each clip is a row of its own that starts from the Rig.

Take the courier from the sample project. Her first row ends at Rig. You want to try a hand-painted texture without losing the baked one, so you branch from Game mesh and add Paint: that's b1/paint. Then you add a walk: that's clips/walk/motion, built on her Rig. Three rows, one asset, and nothing on the first row changed.

Stale: built from something you no longer use​

Every take records its parents: the takes that were in use on the step before it when it ran. Use a different take of the courier's Turnaround, and her High poly, Game mesh and Rig were all built from the old one. They're stale. The b1 Paint and the walk come after those steps, so they need to run again too.

Stale isn't an error and nothing is lost. It means "this doesn't match what you chose any more". Run the steps again and they match. Use the old take again and nothing is stale.

Some steps stop for a review before the rest runs, so a long chain doesn't build on something you haven't looked at: a character's Rig, a sound or a track after its first step, text before it's translated.

What it means in practice​

  • You can change your mind at any step. The cost is running the steps after it again, not starting over.
  • Try a different finish on a path, and keep both.
  • Export refuses an asset with stale steps, so Godot never gets a rig from one mesh and a texture from another.
  • Deleting a step takes the steps after it, and the paths and clips leaving from them, to the project's .trash/ folder. The first step can't be deleted.
  • On the command line a step goes by its id: Game mesh is retopo, Rig is skin, a branch step is b1/paint.
