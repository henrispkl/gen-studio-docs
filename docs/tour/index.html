A tour of the app

Five screens and four words. Read this once, then make your first prop.

The four words​

  • An asset is one thing you make: a prop, a character, a sound.
  • A step is one stage of making it: Concept, Turnaround, Game mesh. An asset is a row of steps.
  • A take is one try at a step. Takes stay until you discard them.
  • The take in use is the one the next step builds from.

Change the take in use, and the steps built from the old one turn stale: still there, but out of date. One click rebuilds them.

Home​

The app opens here. It answers "what's happening, and what needs me?"

  • The prompt bar makes something straight away. Pick a kind, write a line, pick a profile, press Make it. All kinds… opens the full list. You can also drop a picture, a model or a sound on it to import that file.
  • Latest takes are the newest result of each asset.
  • Waiting for you lists what can't go on without you: failures, reviews, stale steps, and finished assets not yet in Godot. Each card has its fix.
  • On the GPU shows the running job, what's next and what's using the graphics memory.
  • Projects switches between your games. A project is a folder.

Assets​

Every asset in the project. Press Assets in the top bar, or Ctrl+2.

The left side filters: by group, or by Needs you, Working, Stale, Favourites and Not exported. Click a card to see its steps on the right, double-click to open it. The bar under each card has one segment per step, in the step's status colour.

New asset​

Ctrl+N, or All kinds… on Home, or New asset on the Assets page. Two questions: what do you start from, and what is it?

From a description makes something new. From a file brings in what you have: a picture, a model, a sound, a shader, or free assets from online libraries. The form on the right shows the steps the kind runs, so you see the path before you start.

An asset​

This is where the work happens. Each open asset is a tab in the top bar.

From the top:

  • The toolbar: the name, Export to Godot, Auto-continue and Run to end. Re-run stale shows when something is stale.
  • The rail: the steps, as pills. A dot shows each one's status. Click one to open it. An asset made from another one (a Shot from its Character) starts with that asset, marked source: its picture to look at, nothing to change there. The dashed + adds a step. Right-click for more: retake, run to here, branch, delete.
  • Inputs (left): what the step builds from, then its form. The profile, the prompt, the settings. Advanced has the rest. Retake at the bottom makes a new take.
  • The tool panel (right, on steps that change something you already have): small previews of what the take came from, this take's tools (on Retouch: the area, find by name, the change), and Build on Take N and Retake. Details folds up at its foot.
  • The take (middle): large. Double-click, or Open large, for the big viewer. The bar above it has the take's tools, and Run next or Go to for the next step.
  • Takes strip (bottom): every take of this step, oldest first. The last card, New take, shows what it starts from, or on a first step the last take for reference.
  • Details (right, on steps with no tool panel): model, seed, time, licence: how this take was made.

By default a new asset runs its first step, then waits. You look at the takes, keep one, and press Run next. Run to end does the rest in one go. Auto-continue runs on by itself until a step that needs review.

The queue​

The bottom bar shows the running job and how busy the machine is. Queue, at its right end, opens the list of what's running and what's waiting. One big model runs at a time, so jobs wait their turn. See the queue.

Settings​

Settings at the top right, or Ctrl+,.

  • Models: what's installed, what fits your GPU, and install or remove.
  • Project: this game's Target look (PS1 to PS4), default profile, and sound levels.
  • Storage: where things live, and what uses the space.
  • Extensions: packs of models and steps, on and off.
  • Appearance: colours, text size, Reduce motion.
  • Setup: the first-run window again.
  • About: the version, the machine check, licences.

Two dials​

Two settings shape every result, and they're separate on purpose:

  • Target look is the era your game looks like, PS1 to PS4. It sets the polygon and texture budgets.
  • Profile is how long a step may take, Ultra fast to Quality. It picks the model.

More in profiles and target looks.

Keys worth learning​

Ctrl+K searches every action and asset. On an asset, [ and ] move between steps, ← and → between takes, R makes a new take, U uses the picked take. F1 opens help on the step you're on. All of them: keyboard shortcuts.
