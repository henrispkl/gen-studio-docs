Edit a composed piece bar by bar

You get a new take of a composed piece with the bars you didn't like redrawn, your own notes where you wanted them, and the rest exactly as it was.

A Composed music asset is written by rules from a style and a seed, with no model: its Compose step takes about 20 seconds on the CPU and gives a .mid for your DAW and a rendered preview. The editor changes that piece one bar, or one instrument in one bar, at a time.

Before you start​

You need a Composed music asset with a Compose take. The examples use the sample piece festival_waltz (style Gentle town).

  • In the app
  • Command line
  1. Open the asset, click Compose on the rail, and press Edit on a take. The strip on top is the piece: a chord card per bar, then a lane per instrument.
  2. Pick what to work on. Click an instrument's notes in a bar to work on that instrument there. Click the chord card to pick the whole bar. Shift-click picks a range. The line under the strip says what's picked, for example Bar 5 · all instruments.
  3. Press New idea for a fresh draw of what's picked. Press Rework rhythm or Rework pitches to vary the notes already there, yours included.
  4. Open the Takes tab. Each idea is listed there, with the notes it changed drawn brighter. Play one in place, pick the one to keep, or go back to Current.
  5. Open the Notes tab to write notes yourself. It is the piano roll of the picked instrument: click to draw, drag to move, drag an end to resize, right-drag to erase. Shift-click on a note stacks another. The drum kit is a step rack instead. The bars either side are shown dimmed.
  6. Press Lock on bars you're happy with. A locked bar is left alone by later re-rolls. The button reads Unlock when everything picked is locked.
  7. Press Space to play from the picked bar.
  8. Press Save as new take. It queues a Compose take from this take's seed and your edits. The take you opened stays as it was.

Nothing changes until Save as new take. Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) work on every edit before that.

Edits go in the Compose step's edit field, separated by ;. They are applied to the newest take and keep its seed:

gen retake festival_waltz compose --set edit="reroll 5; lock 3"
gen retake festival_waltz compose --set edit="reroll_rhythm 7; reroll_pitch 8"
gen retake festival_waltz compose --set edit="give 5 auto C5:8,E5:8"
gen retake festival_waltz compose --set edit="reroll_part 5 kit; volume nylon -3"

The number is a bar's id. Bar ids are in the take's piece.json.

ActionWhat it does
reroll 5a new draw of bar 5
reroll_rhythm 5, reroll_pitch 5a new rhythm, or new pitches, for bar 5
lock 3, unlock 3keep bar 3 out of re-rolls, or let it back in
motif 7make bar 7 the motif
insert 5, insert end, delete 5add a bar before bar 5 or after the last bar, or remove bar 5
give 5 auto C5:8,E5:8your own bar, as typed notes
give_midi 5 file.midyour own bar, from a MIDI file
reroll_part 5 kita new draw of one instrument in bar 5
rework 5 nylon rhythma variation of one instrument's notes (rhythm or pitch)
give_part 5 kit 0:1:36,8:1:38your notes for one instrument, as on:length:pitch
sound nylon 4, volume nylon -3, pan nylon -40a part's General MIDI sound, its volume in dB, its pan

kit and nylon are part names from a bossa nova piece. Yours depend on the style. A plain gen retake with no edit is a new piece, not an edit.

Variations​

  • A new idea over your own notes: a new idea for a whole bar asks first when the bar has notes of yours. Keep my notes, re-roll the rest leaves them.
  • Cue and loop: the ruler over the strip works like a DAW's. Click it to play from that beat. Drag along it to mark a span that Play loops. A click clears the span. With no span, the loop button repeats the picked bars. A line shows what's playing.
  • Step through bars: the Left and Right arrow keys pick the bar before or after. Delete removes the notes picked in the roll.
  • More room for notes: Full screen gives the roll the lanes' room. Back to all instruments returns.
  • Sound, volume and pan: click an instrument's notes, then use the channel in the side panel. It gives the instrument another General MIDI sound (the kit: another drum kit), a volume and a pan. The instrument keeps its part in the music: only how it's played changes.
  • Chords: the side panel lists the chords that fit the picked bar, best first. Click one to use it. Under them are Set as motif, Insert after, Copy to… and Delete.
  • Longer or shorter sections: the − and + on a section's name remove or add a bar at its end.
  • Everything at once: Re-roll every unlocked bar redraws all bars you haven't locked.
  • Limits: an edit never changes another bar, so the bars either side of an insert or a delete keep their accompaniment. The piece is rendered through a General MIDI SoundFont. The .mid is what you take to a DAW for better sounds.
