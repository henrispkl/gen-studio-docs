Game assets on your own GPU.

Gen Studio turns a sentence into concept art, 3D models, rigs, animation, sound, voices, text and anime art, then puts them in your Godot project. It all runs on your machine.

Get started See what it makes

How it works

One asset, from a sentence to the game: a herbalist for a small forest game.

  1. Describe itWrite a sentence. Or start from a picture, a model, a video of you moving, or a free asset.
  2. Pick a takeEach step keeps every try. Compare them, use the best, retake the rest.
  3. Run the stepsConcept, four views, 3D, game mesh, rig, clips. Each in your target look's budget.
  4. Export to GodotModels, animations, wardrobes, shaders, sounds and 2D rigs, ready to use.

What you can make

The same list as the app's New asset window.

Characters & bodies

3D game characterConcept, turnaround, 3D, game mesh and a skeleton to review; then skin and clips. WearableArmour, clothing or an accessory for a rigged character, fitted and skinned to it. Base bodyA plain body (underwear, a bodysuit or a mannequin) that wardrobe pieces fit onto.

Props & scenery

3D propFrom a prompt: concept art, a cut-out, then a low-poly model checked in Godot. Texture tileA seamless tile at the target look, with its maps and a Godot material.

Effects

EffectFire, smoke, sparks or magic: a sprite, then a flipbook animated by a video model.

Anime (a pack, off by default)

Anime imageAn Anima drawing with its background: any characters, framing and shape. Anime characterDefine a character once (a full-body sheet and their tags); then shoot them as often as you like. Shot of a characterA character in any framing (full body to head), cut out, on white or in a scene. Sprite or portrait setOne shot in every expression, with talking and blinking, a faceset, and Ren'Py and Godot files. BackgroundA scene with nobody in it, at other times of day (same layout), with optional parallax layers. Reactions (chibi or full size)A whole drawing per expression, cut out: lively, not lined up; for chat, menus or a desktop chibi. 2D rig (cut-out animation)A transparent shot split into parts on Godot bones: breathing, sway and a head tilt, hair that swings.

Pixel art & chiptune

Pixel spriteA prop, creature or object as true pixels with a limited palette. Pixel characterA full-body character as true pixels. Pixel iconA UI or item icon, 32 px by default. Pixel tilesA sheet of tiles cut at their borders, 16 or 32 px, with one palette. ChiptuneA chip-flavoured track from a description. To hear a track on real chip voices: Stems, MIDI, then add Chip render. Retro sound effectCoins, jumps, lasers and explosions, made by a generator in a moment.

Sound

Sound effectRound-robin variants of one sound, trimmed, levelled and given the target look's era. MusicA looping track at the project's loudness and sample rate. VoiceA character's voice from a description, frozen as the reference for its lines.

Writing

ItemName, descriptions and flavour text as checked JSON. DialogueA scene of lines with stable keys, as a Godot Dialogue Manager file; then translations. BarksShort lines for a character in idle, combat, hurt and victory; then translations. NamesA list of names with meanings, in the style you describe. Lore checkAny text compared with your lore files: contradictions and unclear claims.

Shader

ShaderA Godot shader written from your description, compiled in Godot until it works.

From a file

Picture to 3DYour own picture of one object, turned into a low-poly model. Character modelYour FBX, OBJ, GLB, VRM or .blend. A rigged humanoid gets clips straight away. Prop modelYour own model as a prop's mesh, ready to texture, paint or export. SoundA sound you have, finished like a made one (trim, era, loudness). MusicA track you have, at the project's loudness and era. ShaderA .gdshader you have, kept with its licence. Find free assets (also AivisHub voices)Models, textures, sounds, music, shaders and voices from free libraries.

Why it works this way

On your machinePrompts, pictures and results stay on your disk. No accounts, no credits, no one else's queue.
Nothing overwrittenA new try is a new take. Go back to any of them. What's out of date is marked, and one click from fixed.
Made again, the sameEvery take records its model revision, seed and settings, and its licence.
The right model per stepEach profile picks a model, from seconds per try to the best your card can run. Models are pinned, and one job holds the GPU at a time.

What you need

Gen Studio is a big install for a big card. Here's the honest part.

Linux and an NVIDIA GPUBuilt and tested on an RTX 4090 with 24 GB. Many steps want 16 to 24 GB. Smaller cards run the Fast profile.
80 to 358 GB of diskMinimal is 80 GB, Recommended 266 GB, Full 358 GB. Setup shows the plan before anything downloads.
Godot 4For your game. Blender on your PATH to import models. Everything else installs itself.
